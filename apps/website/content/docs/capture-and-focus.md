Two small features that pull in opposite directions: one for getting something out of your head — or finding anything — fast, one for staying with a single thing.

## The command palette

On the desktop app, press **⌥Space** (**Alt+Shift+Space** on Windows and Linux, where plain Alt+Space is the window menu) from anywhere — Companion doesn't even need to be the app you're looking at. A command palette drops in over your work, the way Spotlight does.

![The command palette open over Today, listing its commands: New task, New note, New canvas, and the find commands](/docs/palette.png)

It opens on its list of commands. Four keys run it:

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| **↑ ↓** | Move through the list |
| **⏎** | Pick the highlighted row |
| **⇧⏎** | Open the highlighted result in a new tab |
| **Esc** | Step back out — and, from the top, close the palette |

Pressing the shortcut again puts the palette away too, as does clicking outside it. The bottom edge of the palette always shows what the keys will do right now, so there's nothing to memorise.

## Capture

**New task** is first in the list, so **⌥Space ⏎** is all it takes to start one. Type the title and press **⏎** to save. The palette closes and you're back in whatever you were doing.

![A new task in the palette, with a project chip focused and the deadline and reminder questions below](/docs/palette-new-task.png)

Want more than a title? Keep going with **Tab**:

- **File it.** **Tab** walks your projects, one chip at a time, and **⏎** on a chip saves the task straight into that project.
- **Date it.** Tab past the chips for a deadline and a reminder. Both take plain language — "friday", "tomorrow 9am", "a day before".
- **Both.** To file a task *and* date it, press **Space** on the project's chip to pick it, then Tab on to the dates.

**New note** takes a title and, a **Tab** away, a body; **⌘⏎** saves from there. **New canvas** names a [board](/docs/your-first-canvas) and opens it, ready to draw on.

A saved task or note stays out of your way. If you'd rather carry on working in it, save with **⇧⏎** instead and it opens in a tab of its own.

## Find

Don't pick a command — just type. Every title is searched at once: notes, tasks, projects, areas, canvases and calendar events. **⏎** opens the hit in Companion, in the tab that already holds it if there is one.

![Typing "launch" into the palette finds matching notes, a canvas, a task and a project, and offers to create something new](/docs/palette-find.png)

The find commands narrow that to one kind of thing — **Find a note**, **Find a task**, **Find a project** and so on. Three of them search by day instead of by title: **Find tasks by date**, **Find calendar events by date** and **Find projects by date** read "today", "friday" or "sep 30" and list what falls on that day. With nothing typed, they show what's coming up. An event opens the calendar on its week.

Typing something that doesn't exist yet? The bottom of the list offers to make it — **New task “…”**, **New note “…”**, **New canvas “…”** — with what you typed as the title.

## Change the shortcut, or go without one

Don't like that chord? **Settings › Shortcuts** rebinds it: click the shortcut, press the keys you want. The binding is per-device and takes effect immediately; if the system won't give up the combination you picked, Companion says so and keeps the old one.

The global shortcut is a desktop feature — a browser tab can't register one. Inside the app, on desktop and web alike, the toolbar's **Capture** button (or **⌥⇧Space**) opens the same palette. To skip its list, **⌥⇧N**, **⌥⇧T** and **⌥⇧C** (Alt+Shift on Windows and Linux) open it straight on a new note, task or canvas — on desktop they're also in the **File** menu.

Captured from inside the app, a new note, task or canvas lands where you're looking: open on a project or an area, it's filed there (a new task starts with that project's chip picked — **Space** unpicks it); anywhere else, it goes to Unsorted. The global shortcut, with no screen behind it, always captures to Unsorted unless you pick a project.

## Focus

The opposite move. Any open tab has an **expand** icon; click it and that note or task pops into a window of its own — no rail, no toolbar, no tabs, no other notes. On desktop that's a real window you can put beside the thing you're writing about; on the web it's a clean browser window.

Close the window and you're back to your workspace, with the item saved.

> **Tip:** Two focus windows side by side is a decent way to draft a note against the meeting notes that prompted it.
