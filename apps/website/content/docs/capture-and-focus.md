Two small features that pull in opposite directions: one for getting something out of your head fast, one for staying with a single thing.

## Quick capture

On the desktop app, press **⌥Space** (**Alt+Shift+Space** on Windows and Linux, where plain Alt+Space is the window menu) from anywhere — Companion doesn't even need to be the app you're looking at. A command palette drops in over your work, the way Spotlight does.

Don't like that chord? **Settings › Shortcuts** rebinds it: click the shortcut, press the keys you want. The binding is per-device and takes effect immediately; if the system won't give up the combination you picked, Companion says so and keeps the old one.

The palette opens on its list of commands. **↑↓** moves, **⏎** picks, **Esc** steps back out (and closes it from the top). Pressing the shortcut again puts it away too, as does clicking outside it.

**Capturing.** **New task** is first in the list, so **⌥Space ⏎** is all it takes to start one: type the title and **⏎** saves. Want it filed? **Tab** walks your projects, one chip at a time, and **⏎** on a chip saves the task straight into that project. Keep tabbing for a deadline and a reminder — plain language, "friday", "tomorrow 9am", "a day before"; to file a task *and* date it, press **Space** on the project's chip to pick it before you Tab on. **New note** takes a title and, a **Tab** away, a body (**⌘⏎** saves from there). **New canvas** names a board and opens it. After a task or a note the palette closes and you're back in whatever you were doing.

**Finding.** Just type: every title is searched at once — notes, tasks, projects, areas, canvases, calendar events — and **⏎** opens the hit in Companion. The find commands narrow that to one kind of thing, and three of them search by day instead of by title: **Find tasks by date**, **Find calendar events by date** and **Find projects by date** read "today", "friday" or "sep 30", and list what falls on that day (with nothing typed, what's coming up). An event opens the calendar on its week.

Typing something that doesn't exist yet? The bottom of the list offers to make it — **New task “…”**, **New note “…”**, **New canvas “…”** — with what you typed as the title.

The global shortcut is a desktop feature — a browser tab can't register one. Inside the app, on desktop and web alike, the toolbar's **Capture** button (or **⌥⇧Space**) opens the same palette.

## Focus

The opposite move. Any open tab has an **expand** icon; click it and that note or task pops into a window of its own — no rail, no toolbar, no tabs, no other notes. On desktop that's a real window you can put beside the thing you're writing about; on the web it's a clean browser window.

Close the window and you're back to your workspace, with the item saved.

> **Tip:** Two focus windows side by side is a decent way to draft a note against the meeting notes that prompted it.
